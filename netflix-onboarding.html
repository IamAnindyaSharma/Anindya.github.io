Netflix: user onboarding

How Netflix turns a new visitor into a paying subscriber, and where momentum is gained or lost along the way.

DomainConsumer subscription / OTTFocusOnboarding, conversion, frictionSkillsUser analysis, flow mapping, metric design
Summary. I walked Netflix's onboarding as a price-sensitive first-time buyer, tracked how the user's mindset changes at each step, and tied each design choice to measurable onboarding metrics.

01Context: product and goal

I started with Netflix as a product and a business: its mission, vision and subscription model. The key insight is that onboarding has one job: reduce friction, build excitement, and get the user to complete sign-up with confidence.

02Primary user persona

  • Who: a young first-year undergraduate student
  • Constraints: limited income, no credit card
  • Mindset: price-sensitive and new to paid OTT platforms

Using this persona kept every onboarding decision grounded in a real user's perspective rather than an abstract one.

03Onboarding flow analysis

STEP 1Landing screen
STEP 2Account creation
STEP 3Plan selection
STEP 4Payment

For every step I evaluated three things:

  • What the screen is trying to achieve
  • How much effort it asks of the user
  • Whether it builds excitement or causes friction

I also tracked the user's psychological state ("psych level") at each stage, to spot where the experience motivates and where users may feel confused, tired or discouraged.

04What works and what to improve

What Netflix does well

  • Engaging visuals on the first screen
  • Minimal information asked upfront
  • "Cancel anytime" messaging that removes fear
  • Multiple payment options, including UPI, which widens access

Where it can improve

  • Unnecessary intermediate steps that slow the flow
  • Email verification friction at a high-intent moment
  • Mergeable screens: combine or remove some to shorten the path

05Key takeaways

  • A strong first impression drives the rest of the flow
  • Reduce resistance at every step of sign-up
  • Flexible payment options expand who can convert
  • An easy exit builds trust and, paradoxically, willingness to start

06Metrics: how onboarding success is measured

I defined a North Star and supporting metrics so UX decisions connect back to business outcomes.

Sign-up rateHow well the landing experience converts interest
Sign-up completion rateHow much friction exists between start and finish
Total users signed upThe overall volume outcome of onboarding

07Product + AI angle: ideas to test

These are hypotheses that follow from the friction points above. They are not tested results.
H1

Defer email verification until after first play, so the user reaches value sooner.

H2

AI-personalised plan suggestion using region, device and payment preference (for example, UPI-first for price-sensitive users in India).

H3

Taste-based preview before payment: a quick pick of genres or titles that shows a tailored catalogue and builds excitement at the point of commitment.

TEST

How I'd test it: A/B test each change, with sign-up completion rate as the primary metric and early retention as a guardrail so conversion gains don't hide weaker intent.

What this project shows

I can break down a real-world product flow, think from the user's mindset, identify friction points, and connect product decisions to business metrics.
